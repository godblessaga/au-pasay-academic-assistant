- Exceeded Residency: Must formally request extension from Admissions Committee.
- Extension Rules: Maximum of two (2) extensions allowed. Approved petitioners must take an additional 3-unit subject per semester (exclusive of summer) starting from the expiration date.

[RETENTION & EVALUATION POLICIES]
- General Retention: Continued study depends on maintaining cumulative General Weighted Average (GWA) including PE and NSTP/ROTC.
- 2nd Year to 3rd Year Promotion (Nursing & Board Programs): Must maintain a minimum GWA of 2.5 (including PE and NSTP) and pass a Panel Interview. Failing GWA requires special remedial classes.
- 3rd Year to 4th Year Promotion: Must maintain a minimum GWA of 2.5 and pass the college-administered Revalida with a minimum grade of 75%.
- Revalida Rules: Revalida score accounts for 20% of the final grade for Course Audit I. Failing the first Revalida grants a second attempt in summer. Failing Course Audit I requires retaking the entire subject.

1. Secure a Shifting Form from the Dean's Office of your current college.
2. Obtain academic clearance and signature from your current College Dean.

4. Obtain approval and signature from the receiving College Dean.
5. Submit the approved Shifting Form to the Registrar's Office to update your official student program record.
6. Proceed to the Dean's Office of your new program to encode your new class schedule.

Jurisdiction & Overview:
- The Office for Student Affairs (OSA), located on the 2nd Floor, College Building, exercises primary jurisdiction over student conduct and minor infractions.
- Major disciplinary cases are elevated to the University Disciplinary Board/Committee for formal investigation and sanction determination.

[1. MINOR OFFENSES]
Minor offenses refer to light infractions against campus decorum and daily regulations.

Examples of Minor Offenses:
- Failure to wear the official student ID or improper wearing of the ID inside campus.
- Non-compliance with the university dress code or wash day guidelines.
- Minor classroom disturbances, loitering along corridors during class hours, or unnecessary noise.
- Littering within campus premises.
- Eating or drinking in prohibited areas (e.g., computer laboratories, library).
- Unauthorized use of mobile phones or electronic gadgets during ongoing classes.

Disciplinary Procedures for Minor Offenses:
- 1st Offense: Verbal warning and reprimand by the faculty member, department chair, or OSA officer.
- 2nd Offense: Written reprimand/warning and mandatory counseling session at the Guidance Office.
- 3rd Offense: Formal written warning, parent/guardian notification, and potential community/campus service assignment.

[2. MAJOR OFFENSES]
Major offenses involve severe violations of university policies, law, or actions that compromise campus safety and institutional integrity.

Examples of Major Offenses:
- Academic dishonesty, including cheating during examinations, forgery, tampering with official school records, or plagiarism.
- Vandalism or intentional destruction/damage of university property, facilities, or IT equipment.
- Physical assault, fighting, brawling, bullying, cyberbullying, harassment, or extortion involving students or school personnel.
- Possession, distribution, or consumption of prohibited drugs, dangerous substances, or illegal items inside or near campus.
- Possession or consumption of alcoholic beverages, or entering the campus under the influence of liquor.
- Possession of deadly weapons, explosives, or hazardous materials.
- Joining, recruiting for, or participating in unauthorized fraternities, sororities, or unauthorized student organizations.
- Using vulgar, obscene, or threatening language/gestures against faculty, administrators, staff, or fellow students.

Disciplinary Procedures & Sanctions for Major Offenses:
- Immediate referral to the Office for Student Affairs (OSA) and filing of formal charges.
- Issuance of a preventive suspension pending formal investigation if campus safety is deemed at risk.
- Formal hearing conducted by the Disciplinary Committee.
- Sanctions depending on severity: Suspension from classes, non-readmission, exclusion, or expulsion from the university.======================================================================

[1. ACADEMIC & PROGRAM TERMINOLOGY]
* Course / Courses = Degree Programs, Academic Programs, Majors, or SHS Strands (e.g., BSN, BSIT, AB English, STEM).
* Program = Official four-year/five-year degree offered by a College or School.
* Major = Field of specialization within a degree program (e.g., BSBA Major in Marketing Management).
* Strand = Specialized track under Senior High School (e.g., HUMSS, STEM, ABM, GAS, TVL).
* Unit / Subject = Individual class or academic subject enrolled per semester.
* Prerequisite = A subject required to be passed before enrolling in an advanced subject.
* Academic Load = Total number of subjects/units a student is enrolled in for a term.
* TCP = Teacher Certificate Program (18-unit program for non-education graduates).

[2. GRADING & EVALUATION TERMINOLOGY]
* GWA = General Weighted Average (cumulative grade performance index).
* INC = Incomplete grade status (requires completion of missing requirements).
* FDA = Failure Due to Absences (dropped status due to unexcused absences).
* Revalida = Comprehensive exit evaluation exam required for 3rd-year promotion in nursing/board programs.
* Course Audit I = Senior-level nursing review course where Revalida accounts for 20% of the final grade.
* Dean's List / Academic Honors = Semester honors requiring a GWA of 1.75 or higher with no grade below 2.00.

[3. ENROLLMENT & ADMINISTRATIVE TERMINOLOGY]
* Enrollment = Process of registration, fee assessment, and fee payment.
* Officially Enrolled = Status achieved after paying fees and obtaining a stamped RAF.
* RAF = Registration and Assessment Form (official printout of classes and tuition assessment).
* SIS = Student Information Sheet filled out during initial admission.
* Shifting = Changing degree programs within AU Pasay.
* Transferring = Admitting to AU Pasay from another college or university.
* Cross-Enrollee = A student taking specific subjects at AU from another school (or vice versa) with a permit.
* Wash Day = Friday civilian attire policy following general dress code rules.

[4. OFFICES, LOCATIONS & ENTITIES]
* OSA = Office for Student Affairs (2nd Floor, College Building; handles discipline, orgs, and discounts).
* Registrar's Office = Handles records, admission credentials, and transcripts (Main Building, Ground Floor).
* Bursar's Office = Cashier/payment processing center (Main Building, Ground Floor).
* IT Center = Handles RAF reprinting and Student ID processing (3rd Floor, College Building).
* PACUCOA = Philippine Association of Colleges and Universities Commission on Accreditation (accrediting agency).
* AUSSC = Arellano University Supreme Student Council.
